Using the shared master context, design the complete Multi-Branch Architecture for the Multi-Tenant SaaS Salon & Spa Management ERP Platform.

The organizational structure must support:

Company
→ Branch
→ Business Unit
→ Department
→ Staff

Example:
A company may have:
- Branch A in City X
- Branch B in City Y

Inside Branch A:
- Men's Salon
- Women's Salon
- Spa Center
- Massage Center

Each business unit may have:
- Separate staff
- Separate services
- Separate inventory
- Separate reports
- Separate queue behavior
- Separate operational settings

But the owner must still see:
- Central dashboard
- Consolidated financial overview
- Branch comparison reports
- Company-wide settings

Design requirements:

1. Company Level
Define:
- Company profile
- Branding
- Subscription ownership
- Global settings
- Default currency and timezone
- Default commission policies
- Default invoice settings
- Default SMS settings

2. Branch Level
Define:
- Branch profile
- Address
- City
- Working hours
- Phone
- Active status
- Branch manager assignment
- Branch-specific settings
- Branch-specific pricing if needed

3. Business Unit Level
Define:
- Business unit type such as men's salon, women's salon, spa, massage, beauty center
- Gender rules if needed
- Assigned branch
- Assigned staff
- Assigned services
- Assigned queue rules
- Assigned inventory stock
- Assigned reporting categories

4. Department Level
Define whether department is necessary and how it differs from business unit.
Examples:
- Hair department
- Nail department
- Facial department
- Massage department
- Cashier department

5. Staff Assignment
Explain:
- Staff can belong to a branch
- Staff can be assigned to one or more business units
- Staff can have skills
- Staff can have roles
- Staff can work across business units if allowed

6. Data Scoping
Explain how data is scoped:
- Owner sees all branches
- Branch manager sees only assigned branch
- Receptionist sees only assigned branch or business unit
- Staff sees assigned services only
- Reports respect scope restrictions

7. Reporting Structure
Design:
- Branch-level reports
- Business-unit-level reports
- Department-level reports
- Staff-level reports
- Consolidated company reports
- Branch comparison reports

8. Operational Independence
Each business unit should be able to have:
- Different service catalog
- Different staff
- Different inventory
- Different opening hours
- Different queue display
- Different receipt notes if needed

9. Central Control
The owner should still be able to:
- Create new branches
- Duplicate services
- Transfer staff
- Compare performance
- Control subscription
- Standardize categories

Provide:
- Suggested database tables and relationships
- Scope rules
- Permission rules
- Example hierarchy
- Edge cases
- Recommended UI navigation structure

Output format:
- Organization model
- ERD impact
- Scoping rules
- Reporting rules
- Example scenarios
- Acceptance criteria
